\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement-learning (RL) detectors with asymmetric rewards have been proposed for safety-prioritized detection, where a missed event costs more than a false alarm. We ask whether such a detector improves cybersickness detection in virtual reality (VR) once evaluation is free of data leakage. E-DDQN is a dueling Double Deep Q-Network whose reward penalizes a missed cybersickness event five times more than a false alarm. On the public UFFCSData gameplay dataset (\nSessionsEval{} sessions, \nSamplesEval{} one-second samples), oversampling before a random row-level split placed exact copies of \LeakDupPct\,\% of the positive test rows in the training data and let an untuned classifier reach ROC-AUC \LeakOldRocAuc; a session-grouped split of the same features gave \LeakGrpRocAuc. We prove that on logged data the alarm cannot affect future observations, so the task is a contextual bandit and the 5:1 reward induces exactly the decision rule of 5:1 cost-sensitive classification. Under a nested, session-grouped protocol fixed before testing, with equal tuning budgets for seven models, E-DDQN had a 5:1 expected cost of \EDDQNCostCostMean{} and ROC-AUC \EDDQNCostRocAucMean. It did not outperform a standard DQN (\DQNCostCostMean, \DQNCostRocAucMean) and had a higher cost than a cost-weighted random forest (\RFCostCostMean, \RFCostRocAucMean; Holm-adjusted group-bootstrap $p=$\,\PHHolmRF). A logistic regression on elapsed time alone, added as a post-hoc baseline, reached the highest ROC-AUC of all models (\PHTimeLRRocAucMean), statistically indistinguishable from the random forest. No model combined high recall with a controlled false-alarm rate: at the cost-optimal thresholds, \DOneFPRMin--\DOneFPRMax\,\% of non-event seconds raised an alarm. Bounded perturbations of the logged telemetry changed ROC-AUC by at most \AttAucMaxAbsChange. The asymmetric reward is a cost-sensitive operating point rather than a new learning objective, and RL detectors should be compared with cost-weighted and exposure-time baselines under participant-independent evaluation.
\end{abstract}
