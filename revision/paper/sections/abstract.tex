\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement-learning (RL) detectors with asymmetric rewards have been proposed for safety-prioritized detection. We ask whether one, E-DDQN---a dueling Double Deep Q-Network whose reward penalizes a missed cybersickness event five times more than a false alarm---improves detection in virtual reality under leakage-controlled evaluation. On logged data the alarm cannot affect future observations, so the task is a contextual bandit and the 5:1 reward induces exactly the 5:1 cost-sensitive Bayes rule. On the public UFFCSData gameplay dataset (\nSessionsEval{} sessions, \nSamplesEval{} one-second samples), consecutive seconds are near-duplicates: random row-level splits give ROC-AUC \LeakRowRocAuc--\LeakOldRocAuc, against \LeakGrpRocAuc{} under session-grouped splits. Under a nested, session-grouped protocol fixed in advance, E-DDQN had a mean 5:1 cost of \EDDQNCostCostMean{} and ROC-AUC \EDDQNCostRocAucMean, against \DQNCostCostMean{} and \DQNCostRocAucMean{} for a standard DQN and \RFCostCostMean{} and \RFCostRocAucMean{} for a cost-weighted random forest (Holm-adjusted $p=$\,\PHHolmRF{} by group bootstrap, \PHSfRFHolm{} by an exact sign-flip test). In two further pre-specified random search draws, E-DDQN never had a significantly lower cost than the standard DQN, but its within-fold ROC-AUC ranged from \SDEDDQNWfMin{} to \SDEDDQNWfMax: its weak primary result reflects its search draw. A post-hoc logistic regression on elapsed time alone reached ROC-AUC \PHTimeLRRocAucMean; no primary model ranked seconds significantly better. Every model's cost-optimal threshold raised alarms on \DOneFPRMin--\DOneFPRMax\,\% of non-event seconds, and bounded telemetry perturbations changed ROC-AUC by up to \AttAucMaxAbsChange. Asymmetric rewards encode a cost-sensitive operating point, not a new learning objective; RL detectors should be compared with cost-weighted and exposure-time baselines, over several search draws, under participant- or session-grouped evaluation.
\end{abstract}
