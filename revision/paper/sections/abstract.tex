\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement-learning (RL) detectors with asymmetric rewards have been proposed for safety-prioritized detection, where a missed event costs more than a false alarm. We ask whether such a detector, E-DDQN---a dueling Double Deep Q-Network whose reward penalizes a missed cybersickness event five times more than a false alarm---improves detection in virtual reality under leakage-controlled evaluation. We show that on logged data the alarm cannot affect future observations, so the task is a contextual bandit and the 5:1 reward induces exactly the 5:1 cost-sensitive Bayes decision rule. On the public UFFCSData gameplay dataset (\nSessionsEval{} sessions, \nSamplesEval{} one-second samples), consecutive samples of a session are near-duplicates: random row-level splits give ROC-AUC \LeakRowRocAuc--\LeakOldRocAuc, against \LeakGrpRocAuc{} under session-grouped splits. Under a nested, session-grouped protocol fixed before testing, E-DDQN had a mean 5:1 expected cost of \EDDQNCostCostMean{} and ROC-AUC \EDDQNCostRocAucMean. It did not outperform a standard DQN (\DQNCostCostMean, \DQNCostRocAucMean) and had a higher cost than a cost-weighted random forest (\RFCostCostMean, \RFCostRocAucMean; seed ensembles \PHEnsEDDQNCost{} vs.\ \PHEnsRFCost; Holm-adjusted $p=$\,\PHHolmRF{} by group bootstrap and \PHSfRFHolm{} by an exact sign-flip test). A post-hoc logistic regression on elapsed time alone reached ROC-AUC \PHTimeLRRocAucMean, and no model ranked seconds significantly better. At the cost-optimal thresholds every model raised alarms on \DOneFPRMin--\DOneFPRMax\,\% of non-event seconds, and bounded telemetry perturbations changed ROC-AUC by at most \AttAucMaxAbsChange. Asymmetric rewards encode a cost-sensitive operating point, not a new learning objective; RL detectors should be compared with cost-weighted and exposure-time baselines under participant- or session-grouped evaluation.
\end{abstract}
