\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We asked whether an asymmetric-reward dueling Double DQN (E-DDQN) improves safety-prioritized cybersickness detection in VR. On the public UFFCSData gameplay dataset we found no evidence that it does. On logged data the detection problem is a contextual bandit, and the 5:1 asymmetric reward induces exactly the Bayes threshold of 5:1 cost-sensitive classification. Under a nested, session-grouped protocol fixed before testing, E-DDQN reached a cost of \EDDQNCostCostMean{} (mean over seeds; seed ensemble \PHEnsEDDQNCost) and ROC-AUC \EDDQNCostRocAucMean, and did not have a significantly lower cost than a standard DQN (\DQNCostCostMean; seed ensemble \PHEnsDQNCost; ROC-AUC \DQNCostRocAucMean). Its seed-ensemble cost was higher than that of a cost-weighted random forest (\RFCostCostMean; seed ensemble \PHEnsRFCost; ROC-AUC \RFCostRocAucMean) in every outer fold, marginally in one; the difference (\PairDOneRFCostDiff, 95\,\% CI \PairDOneRFCostLo{} to \PairDOneRFCostHi) was significant under the primary group bootstrap (Holm-adjusted $p=$\,\PHHolmRF) but not under the exact sign-flip sensitivity analysis ($p=$\,\PHSfRFHolm), so it is not firmly established and does not show that the random forest is superior. A re-tuning with two further random search draws, specified before it was run, found no draw in which E-DDQN had a significantly lower cost than the standard DQN, and in ranking the standard DQN was better with the primary draw (unadjusted $p=$\,\SDPairOneAucP, approximate) and E-DDQN with another ($p=$\,\SDPairTwoAucP); it also suggests that sensitivity to the search draw contributes to E-DDQN's weak primary result, without isolating a single cause: across draws its within-fold ROC-AUC ranged from \SDEDDQNWfMin{} to \SDEDDQNWfMax{} and its cost from \SDEDDQNCostMin{} to \SDEDDQNCostMax, still above the random forest's as a point estimate. A post-hoc logistic regression on elapsed time alone reached ROC-AUC \PHTimeLRRocAucMean, no primary model ranked seconds significantly better, and no model combined high recall with a controlled false-alarm rate or was shown, with linkage groups as the unit of inference, to improve on always alarming at the 5:1 ratio, which reflects the limited evidence rather than demonstrated equivalence. Session grouping was essential to these conclusions: splits that share sessions between training and test give near-perfect scores on these data, whereas session-grouped evaluation of the same features gives ROC-AUC \LeakGrpRocAuc. In a secondary feature-space stress test, which does not evaluate real attacks, bounded telemetry perturbations changed ROC-AUC by at most \AttAucMaxAbsChange, which is not evidence of robustness: most selected configurations use exposure-type inputs, and an adaptive adversary still changed decisions at fixed thresholds. Progress on this problem is likely to require datasets with physiological or eye-tracking signals, verified participant identifiers and, for adversarial claims, manipulations applied to participants with measured effects, and evaluations that include grouped splits, trivial and exposure-time baselines, cost-weighted comparators and more than one random search draw per model.
