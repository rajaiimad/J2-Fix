\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We asked whether an asymmetric-reward dueling Double DQN (E-DDQN) improves safety-prioritized cybersickness detection in VR; on the public UFFCSData gameplay dataset we found no evidence that it does. On logged data the problem is a contextual bandit, and the 5:1 reward induces exactly the Bayes threshold of 5:1 cost-sensitive classification. Under a nested, session-linkage-grouped protocol fixed before testing, E-DDQN reached a 5:1 cost of \EDDQNCostCostMean{} (mean over seeds; seed ensemble \PHEnsEDDQNCost) and ROC-AUC \EDDQNCostRocAucMean, and did not have a significantly lower cost than a standard DQN (\DQNCostCostMean; ensemble \PHEnsDQNCost) in any of three search draws. Its seed-ensemble cost was higher than a cost-weighted random forest's (\RFCostCostMean; ensemble \PHEnsRFCost; difference \PairDOneRFCostDiff, 95\,\% CI \PairDOneRFCostLo{} to \PairDOneRFCostHi), significantly under the primary group bootstrap (Holm-adjusted $p=$\,\PHHolmRF) but not under the sign-flip sensitivity analysis ($p=$\,\PHSfRFHolm), so this difference is not firmly established. Across draws E-DDQN's within-fold ROC-AUC ranged from \SDEDDQNWfMin{} to \SDEDDQNWfMax, which suggests that search-draw sensitivity contributes to its weak primary result without isolating a single cause. A logistic regression on elapsed time alone reached ROC-AUC \PHTimeLRRocAucMean, no primary model ranked seconds significantly better, and no model combined high recall with a controlled false-alarm rate or was shown, with linkage groups as units, to improve on always alarming, which reflects limited evidence rather than demonstrated equivalence. Row-level splits of the same data give near-perfect scores, so grouped evaluation was essential. Bounded perturbations, a secondary feature-space stress test that does not evaluate real attacks, changed ROC-AUC by at most \AttAucMaxAbsChange. RL detectors for this task should be compared with cost-weighted and exposure-time baselines, over several search draws, under grouped evaluation.
