\section{Post-hoc analyses}
\label{sec:s-posthoc}
All analyses in this section were specified after the primary results were known. They use the splits, inner folds, selection objective, checkpoint and threshold rules, cross-fitting recipe and final seeds of the primary protocol, and no primary result was changed. They differ in when they were specified. The exposure-time baselines (S5.1), the Holm adjustment of the comparisons with E-DDQN (S5.2), the comparison with always alarming (S5.3), the grouping sensitivity analyses (S5.4) and the cost curves (S5.5) were committed (\texttt{configs/\allowbreak posthoc.json}) before they were run. The paired comparisons of the exposure-time baselines with the primary models (main text, Section~V-C), the Holm adjustment over the nine models of the comparison with always alarming (S5.3) and the Q-value scale (S5.6) were added while the results were produced and written up, without prior commitment, as were two descriptive additions: the label-dynamics summary (S1.2) and the seed-ensemble block of the main results table. Sections~S5.7--S5.10 were added after a pre-submission review, when all other results were known (key \texttt{review\_round\_2}); they use only the saved per-sample predictions, selected configurations and search logs. Section~S5.11 was prompted by the same review but was specified, with its decision rules, and committed before any of its models was trained; like the exposure-time baselines of S5.1, it trains new models under the full protocol. Section~S5.12, a descriptive calculation that trains no model, was added in the final pre-submission check. As in the main text (Section~III-G), bootstrap $p$-values between 0.01 and 0.05 in the tables of this section are approximate, and a difference whose confidence interval includes zero is a point estimate only.

\subsection{Search-draw sensitivity (specified before it was run)}
\label{sec:s-searchdraw}
Prompted by the dependence of E-DDQN's result on its single random draw (S5.10), this analysis was specified in \texttt{configs/\allowbreak posthoc.json} (key \texttt{search\_draw\_sensitivity}), together with its decision rules, and committed before any of its models was trained. E-DDQN and the standard DQN were each tuned again under the full protocol---same splits, inner folds, search training seed, budget of 16 configurations, selection rule, checkpoint rule, cross-fitting, five final seeds and threshold rules---with two further random draws from the full search space (configuration seeds 9120 and 9130 for E-DDQN, 9122 and 9132 for DQN) and with one draw of 16 configurations restricted to the exposure feature set (9200 and 9202). Perturbation variants were not scored. Draw~1 is the primary analysis and is reproduced exactly. Every variant is reported; none replaces the primary results. The decision rules were: (R1) the statement that E-DDQN does not outperform the standard DQN is challenged if, in any pair, E-DDQN's seed-ensemble cost is lower and the 95\,\% group-bootstrap interval of the difference excludes zero; (R2) E-DDQN's result is called draw-dependent if its seed-mean cost or its within-fold ROC-AUC spans more than 0.05 over its three full-space draws; (R3) under the exposure-only restriction, a within-fold ROC-AUC of E-DDQN within 0.03 of DQN's supports the feature-set explanation of Section~V-G (iii), one more than 0.05 below DQN's means that this explanation is insufficient, and anything in between is inconclusive.

Per variant: number of exposure-only candidates in the draw, number of outer folds whose selected configuration uses the questionnaire indicators (Q folds), 5:1 cost (rule D1; mean $\pm$ SD over seeds), seed-ensemble cost, pooled and within-fold ROC-AUC (seed means), and the discount factor selected in each outer fold.

\subsection{Score scale of the untuned rule D0}
\label{sec:s-scorescale}
Descriptive; added in the final pre-submission check; no model is trained (\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak 14\_score\_scale.py}). The implied probability $\hat p$ (main text, Eqs.~5 and~7) is on the scale of $P(y=1\mid s)$ only for a model trained on the natural class mix with a squared or logistic loss. Class-balanced replay (E-DDQN, E-CB: a fraction $\rho$ of non-events in every minibatch) and the class-balanced minibatches of the CS-MLP (50/50) train on a different class mix; the scores then estimate the posterior under that mix, whose odds are $k=\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}\cdot\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}$ times those of $P(y=1\mid s)$ ($k=\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}$ for 50/50 minibatches), where $\pi$ is the event prevalence of the inner training split. The cut-off $\hat p\ge 1/6$ of rule D0 is then $P(y=1\mid s)\ge 1/(1+5k)$. For the standard DQN, the Huber loss (threshold 1) with the $\pm1$ reward makes $\hat p$ a nonlinear function of $P(y=1\mid s)$; at $\gamma=0$ the minimiser of the expected loss is $Q(s,1)=-Q(s,0)=(2p-1)/\max(p,1-p)$, so $\hat p=1/6$ at $P(y=1\mid s)=\SSDQNHuberCut$ (no closed form for $\gamma>0$, marked --). Logistic regression, gradient boosting, the random forest and the CS-MLP with uniform minibatches are trained on the natural class mix and keep the nominal cut-off $1/6$ (up to calibration). The table gives, for each selected configuration, $\rho$, the mean training prevalence $\pi$ and $k$ over the three inner splits, and the effective cut-off on $P(y=1\mid s)$ (mean and range over the three inner models; the main text quotes the range over all folds). Only rule D0 and the greedy policies of the reward-ratio analysis depend on this scale; rule D1 and all ranking metrics do not.
