\section{Verification of the evaluation boundaries and of the replay buffer}
\label{sec:s-verify}
\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak 15\_pipeline\_verification.py} checks the implementation on the real folds of the evaluation set, using the production code; it trains no reported model. \PVChecksPass{} of \PVChecks{} checks pass (\texttt{results/\allowbreak qc/\allowbreak pipeline\_verification.md}).

\subsection{Splits and duplicated rows}
For the outer split and for all \PVSplitPairs{} pairs of partitions of the 15 outer~$\times$~inner splits (inner training, inner validation, outer test), no session, linkage group or exact questionnaire profile occurs in two partitions, each linkage group lies in exactly one outer fold, the inner folds partition exactly the development groups, and the saved split files equal the recomputed splits. No raw telemetry row, with or without elapsed time, and no derived feature vector of an evaluation partition is identical to a row of the corresponding training partition.

As a descriptive check (no pass/fail criterion), the median distance in the scaled telemetry feature space from an outer-test row to its nearest development row is \PVNNTestMin--\PVNNTestMax{} across outer folds, against \PVNNOtherMin--\PVNNOtherMax{} from a development row to the nearest row of another linkage group and \PVNNOwnMin--\PVNNOwnMax{} to the nearest other row of its own session. Test rows are thus about as close to the training data as unrelated groups are to each other; in two folds they are about as close as rows of the same session. Because rows of one session are only modestly closer to each other than rows of different groups in this feature space, this distance discriminates weakly: it gives no sign of near-duplicated rows across the boundary, but it cannot exclude that a test group contains the same person as a training group. An earlier version of this check required the test-to-training distance to exceed the distance between consecutive seconds; that criterion was ill-posed (a nearest neighbour among about 7,000 rows is naturally closer than one fixed neighbour), failed in four of five folds, and was replaced by the two reference distances above.

\subsection{Training-only fitting and selection}
For every one of the 15 outer~$\times$~inner splits, the learned preprocessing parameters (frame-rate imputation value, acceleration-spike threshold, scaler centres and scales) and the training matrix are unchanged when every telemetry value of the validation and test rows is replaced by noise. The preprocessing used during hyper-parameter search contains no outer-test rows, and none of the \PVHPORuns{} search runs stored a test score. Every selected configuration is the best configuration by pooled inner-validation cost (tie-break PR-AUC), as recomputed from the search log; every D1 threshold, per seed and for the seed ensemble, is recomputed exactly from the saved inner-validation scores; and in all \PVCheckpointRuns{} final training runs of the neural models the checkpoint is the best inner-validation epoch, with no test metric in any training log.

\subsection{Replay transitions}
E-DDQN, E-CB and the standard DQN were trained with each outer fold's selected configuration on the first inner split (\PVReplayRuns{} runs, shortened to two epochs), and every transition written to the replay buffer was logged (\PVTransitions{} transitions in total). In every run: the stored reward equals $R(a,y)$ for the stored action $a$ and the label $y$ of the stored state; every non-terminal next state is the next second of the same session; the terminal flag is set exactly at the last second of each session; and the buffer holds exactly the logged states, actions, rewards, next states and flags, so no transition joins two sessions and no action is overwritten after its reward is computed. The training rows of each session are contiguous and in temporal order. With full-information replay, half of the stored transitions carry the action that was not executed, each with its own reward (Section~III-E of the paper); no stored transition pairs one action with the reward of another.

\subsection{What these checks do not show}
They show that the evaluation is session- and linkage-group-disjoint and that every learned quantity comes from training or inner-validation data. They do not show that the evaluation is participant-independent: the release contains no participant identifiers, and a person who answered the questionnaire differently in two non-consecutive sessions would not be linked.
